The guide's competency grid lists 6.1-6.9, but the Pupil's Book grid on p. ix lists ten levels (6.10). The guide does not define a 6.10. Its syllabus table and detailed sections give slightly different period counts for some levels; the detailed sections (pp. 1-26) are used above. Total: **about 175 periods** a year.

## Prerequisite logic
Grammar builds in this order: be-verbs (Unit 1), present continuous (Unit 2), simple present and question words (Unit 6), modals and possession (Unit 8), pronouns and adverbs (Unit 9), simple past (Unit 10). Vocabulary and description build in a second line: place prepositions (Unit 3), determiners and adjectives (Unit 4), time prepositions (Unit 7). Punctuation (Unit 5) builds on writing simple sentences from Unit 1. Pronunciation (C.L. 1.1) and reading aloud (C.L. 1.2) recur in nearly every unit and are treated as ongoing skills rather than prerequisites.

## Assessment suggestions (summarised from the Teachers' Guide)
- Prefer continuous, informal, formative assessment over one-off tests; keep a progress profile per student and pass it on to the next teacher.
- Treat capitals and punctuation (C.L. 2.x) as must-check items, because errors there tend to stick.
- For listening and speaking (C. 1, 3, 8), use tasks such as listen and draw, listen and fill a grid, role plays and short talks.
- A monthly class "English Literary Association" (spelling games, storytelling, reading aloud, role plays, poems) works as informal performance assessment.
- For the app: MCQ for grammar forms and vocabulary; short-answer items for comprehension; ordering items for jumbled sentences and process steps; teacher-scored tasks for writing and speaking.
