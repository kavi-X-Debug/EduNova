**Reform status.** Sri Lanka planned to bring in new Grade 6 modules in 2026. In January 2026 Cabinet put the Grade 6 reform back to 2027, after the controversy over the Grade 6 English module. Grade 6 students in 2026 are taught the previous syllabus with the previous textbooks. Checks made in November 2026 found no reformed Grade 6 Sinhala module on edupub.gov.lk or nie.lk. The textbook and guide above are therefore the current official materials for 2026, though they are pre-reform. They follow the 2015 competency-based syllabus, which the guide calls the "new syllabus", and edupub's label "නව විෂය නිර්දේශය" refers to that 2015 syllabus.

**Text extraction.** Both PDFs use a legacy (FM-Abhaya-style) Sinhala font. Their text was converted to Unicode with an approximate glyph map (scratch script, not committed). Unit titles were checked against the converted body text. A few titles come from colloquial or Vedda-language phrases and could not be fully confirmed: Unit 3 *චුචෙං චුලඟෙං යමු කිරි නෑනෝ* and Unit 13 *අග්ගළා කංඩෝං පුතායි*. A native reader should confirm their spelling against the printed book.

Page references below use **textbook printed page numbers** (PDF page = printed page + 10). Teacher's guide references use **PDF page numbers**.

The syllabus allocates about **155 periods** in total across 32 competency levels. Levels 1.5, 3.2, 6.3 and 6.4 are skipped in the Grade 6 numbering, and the grid has no entries for them.

## 3. Competencies (syllabus, summarised)

The 12 subject competencies in the guide, paraphrased:

1. Use listening and speaking skills effectively to communicate (levels 1.1-1.4, 1.6).
2. Use reading habits meaningfully to enrich life experience (2.1 reading aloud, 2.2 styles, 2.3 silent reading).
3. Know the Sinhala alphabet and use it correctly in writing (3.1 alphabet, 3.3 spelling).
4. Write correctly using technical conventions (4.1 punctuation, 4.2 word division).
5. Show vocabulary and knowledge of language tradition and usage (5.1 synonyms, antonyms and adjectives, 5.2 proverbs).
6. Write following standard grammar rules (6.1 nouns, 6.2 verbs, 6.5 sentence building).
7. Use language clearly for practical writing (7.1 personal letters, 7.2 greeting cards).
8. Write creatively and clearly (8.1 topic essays, 8.2 wall newspaper and magazine articles).
9. Use language to build culture, values, coexistence and national pride (9.1 tank heritage, 9.2 good customs, 9.3 patriotic songs, 9.4 Vedda culture).
10. Use knowledge from literary, scientific and technical writing (10.1 writer biography, 10.2 food technology).
11. Develop appreciation of literature (11.1 human experience in literature, 11.2 folk poetry).
12. Write creatively from experience (12.1 poems, 12.2 fiction, 12.3 dialogue).

The learning outcomes for each level are summarised in plain English in `path.json` → `outcomes`.

## 4. Prerequisites and teaching order

The textbook mixes literature readings with embedded language skills, so the learning path keeps the book order but splits units that cover clearly separate competencies. That gives 31 concepts from 21 units. The main dependency chains are:

- **Script and grammar:** alphabet and pili (U4) → nouns (U5) → adjectives (U7) and verbs and word classes (U9) → sentence building (U10) → punctuation (U12) → essays (U11) → wall newspapers and magazines (U20).
- **Spelling:** alphabet (U4) → aspirated and prenasalised letters (U8).
- **Reading:** reading aloud (U7) → styled reading (U8) → silent reading (U12) → biography (U17), traditional food (U13) and radio listening (U20).
- **Poetry:** poem appreciation (U2) → writing poems (U2), good-customs poem (U6), folk poems (U14), patriotic song (U18) → classical poetry (U15).
- **Narrative:** folk-tale listening (U1) → proverbs (U16) and folk-tale reading (U19) → creative fiction (U19) → dialogue (U21).
- **Practical writing:** topical speech (U3) → greeting cards → personal letters.

## 5. Assessment suggestions

Each guide page ends with assessment criteria for that competency level. Paraphrased, they ask whether the pupil can retell, explain, classify, use correctly and appreciate. For the app:

- **Quiz items** (mcq or short answer) suit the grammar and spelling concepts: classifying nouns and verbs by number, gender, person and tense; choosing between ණ/න and ළ/ල; aspirated letters; punctuation marks; synonyms and antonyms; proverb meanings.
- **Comprehension** items suit the reading units: sequencing events, finding the theme, explaining word meanings, identifying similes in classical verses.
- **Teacher-assessed or open tasks** suit speaking, reading aloud and creative writing (talks, reading aloud with good voice control, poems, stories, dialogues, letters, greeting cards, wall-newspaper articles). The app can collect drafts and use rubric checklists: layout followed, ideas organised, spelling and punctuation, creativity.
- Mastery should not depend only on auto-marked quizzes for competencies 1, 2, 8 and 12.

## 6. Gaps and uncertainties

- **No reformed (2026/2027) Grade 6 Sinhala module was available from official sites.** The path follows the 2015 syllabus and the 2014/2019 textbook. It will need rebuilding when the reform starts, currently expected in 2027.
- **Terms are estimated.** Neither the guide nor the textbook assigns units to terms.
- **Legacy-font PDFs.** Titles and outcomes were derived from machine-converted text. Two unit titles (Units 3 and 13) and some rare characters may contain conversion errors.
- Teacher's guide references are PDF page numbers. The printed page numbering in the guide restarts in each section.
- Some syllabus readings appear only as teacher reference material (for example *Vatu Kirilli* for level 1.1 and the *Narī Bænā* drama for level 2.2). The textbook includes extracts where relevant.
- The Tamil-medium counterpart is a different subject (Second Language Sinhala). It is recorded in `manifest.json` as a link only.
