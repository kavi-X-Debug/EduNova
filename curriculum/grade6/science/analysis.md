**Page conventions.**
- Textbook page numbers below are the printed numbers. PDF page = printed page + 14.
- Teachers' Guide ("TG") page numbers are its printed numbers. PDF page = printed page + 24 for the guidance section. The syllabus pages use roman numerals xiii-xxii.

**Unit structure.** Every textbook chapter follows the same pattern: activities and numbered assignments, then a summary, exercises and a technical-terms glossary (English/Sinhala/Tamil). Most chapters also end with a project.

## 3. Learning outcomes by unit (summarised in my own words)

1. **Living world.** Tell living from non-living things using life characteristics: growth, reproduction, movement, respiration, nutrition and response. Sort organisms into plants, animals and microorganisms. Explain the main plant-animal differences (locomotion, mode of nutrition, growth pattern). Use a simple dichotomous key. Record and display observation data. Show care for living things.
2. **Things around us.** Separate matter from energy. Name the three states of matter. Compare their shape and volume qualitatively. Test solids for hardness, malleability, ductility, elasticity, brittleness and texture. Link each property to everyday uses of materials.
3. **Water.** Name the three states of water. Describe ground water, rain (precipitation) and surface water as water sources. Classify water as fresh, brackish or sea water by salinity, and compare salt content by experiment. Explain why water matters for life and human activities, and why it is a limited resource.
4. **Energy.** Describe energy as the ability to do work. Identify the Sun as the main energy source. Describe other sources and their uses: biomass, fossil fuels, wind, hydropower, waves, tides, geothermal and nuclear. Build simple models to show some of these sources working. Recognise that some sources run out, and value using energy sustainably.
5. **Light.** State what is needed for vision. Classify objects as luminous or non-luminous, and materials as transparent, translucent or opaque. Distinguish a beam from a ray in diagrams. Show by activities that light travels in straight lines. Describe uses of light: sight, illumination, signalling, communication, medicine, entertainment and photosynthesis.
6. **Sound.** Identify sound as what the ear senses. Classify sounds as natural or artificial. Tell music from noise. Make a simple instrument using a vibrating string, plate or air column. Use sound considerately.
7. **Magnets.** Show that magnets attract some materials. Identify the poles. Know that like poles repel and unlike poles attract. Find poles with a compass. Compare magnet strength. Use repulsion to tell a magnet from a piece of magnetic material.
8. **Electricity.** List everyday uses of electricity. Name devices that generate it, such as dynamos, power stations and cells. Build circuits with a cell, switch, bulb, wires and ammeter. Draw circuits with standard symbols. Test materials for conduction. Show what diodes, LEDs, resistors and LDRs do. Handle electricity safely and save energy.
9. **Heat.** List uses of heat. Generate heat from fuel, electricity, sunlight and friction. Use a thermometer correctly. Demonstrate the effects of heat: temperature rise, expansion, change of state, change of colour, and changes in products. Relate these effects to the environment and to safe handling.
10. **Food interactions.** Classify animals as herbivores, carnivores or omnivores. Explain that all animals depend directly or indirectly on plants. Build food chains from observations. Combine food chains into food webs, and pick out chains within a web. Value the balance of nature.
11. **Weather and climate.** Distinguish weather from climate. Name the factors that determine weather: rain, wind, temperature and humidity. Read and record weather data over a period. Build simple instruments such as a rain gauge, wind vane, anemometer and hygrometer. Describe floods, cyclones, droughts and landslides, and how to prepare for them.

## 4. Prerequisites between units (my analysis; the guide does not state them)

- **1 Living world.** No prerequisites. This is the entry point.
- **2 Things around us.** No prerequisites. It introduces matter, energy and states of matter.
- **3 Water** needs 2, because the states of water build on the states of matter.
- **4 Energy** needs 2, because it builds on the matter vs energy distinction.
- **5 Light** needs 4, because light is a form of energy and the Sun is the main source.
- **6 Sound** needs 4, because sound is a form of energy.
- **7 Magnets** needs 2, because testing materials links to the properties of matter.
- **8 Electricity** needs 4, 7 and 2:
  - 4: energy sources drive generators.
  - 7: magnets are used in dynamos.
  - 2: conductors and insulators link to material properties.
- **9 Heat** needs 2, 4 and 8:
  - 2: change of state.
  - 4: energy sources.
  - 8: electricity as a heat source.
- **10 Food interactions** needs 1 and 4:
  - 1: modes of nutrition of plants and animals.
  - 4: the Sun as the original energy source.
- **11 Weather and climate** needs 3 and 9:
  - 3: rain and the states of water.
  - 9: temperature and thermometers.

## 5. Assessment suggestions

**From the TG.**
- Every competency section ends with assessment criteria. Most are performance-based: presentations, group work, exhibits and constructed devices. They are judged on design, use of materials, process, cost and whether the device works.
- The TG gives three exemplar school-based assessment tools (TG pp. 21-26):
  - **Term 1**: rubric for group work on the properties of solids (competency level 2.3). It scores contribution, cooperation, staying on task and communication on a 4-level scale: beginning, developing, accomplished, exemplary.
  - **Term 2**: making a sound-producing instrument (competency level 3.8/3.9).
  - **Term 3**: field observation and portfolio of animals' feeding habits, with food chains and webs (food-interaction levels).
- The TG pp. 27-28 list 25 recommended practicals. Examples: field study of organisms, a microscope look at pond water, testing properties of solids, a salt-content comparison, energy-source models, light-ray activities, instrument making, circuits with LEDs and LDRs, a heat-effects demonstration, and weather instruments.

**For the app.**
- Use short auto-marked quizzes for the knowledge outcomes: classifying organisms, states of matter, luminous vs non-luminous objects, circuit symbols, food chains, and weather vs climate.
- Use guided offline "try this at home" tasks with self-report for the practical outcomes.
- Run a cumulative revision quiz at each term end. This matches the three term-test points.
- Do not set questions on the textbook's "For your extra knowledge" boxes. The textbook introduction says these are not for term tests.

## 6. Gaps and uncertainties

- **Reformed (2026/2027) Grade 6 Science module not obtained.** No official copy was reachable:
  - nie.lk returned Cloudflare 503 or 404.
  - channelnie.nie.ac.lk is not on the allowed list and returned 522.
  - Copies seen in search results were only on unofficial mirrors (pastpapers.wiki), which I did not download.

  Search listings suggest the reformed first-term module covers "Things Around Us" and "Wonders of the Living World" (unverified). The path will need re-checking once the reformed module is officially published for 2027.
- **Guide source.** The TG was not downloaded from nie.lk itself. It came from the Ministry of Education's e-thaksalawa site (moe.gov.lk), where it is listed as "Teacher's Instruction Manual (New Syllabus)". The document is NIE's own 2015 Teachers' Guide.
- **Numbering inconsistencies inside the TG.**
  - The syllabus numbers the electricity levels 3.12-3.14 and the heat levels 3.15-3.17. The Teaching Sequence (p. 29) folds conductors/insulators in without a number and renumbers heat as 3.14-3.16.
  - Assessment Sample 3 refers to the food levels as 4.3/4.4, while the syllabus has 4.1/4.2.
  - The TG introduction says the syllabus starts "from the year 2025", which is evidently a typo for 2015.
  - This analysis follows the syllabus numbering.
- **Unit placement.** Food interactions (unit 10) appear under Competency 4 (earth and space) in the syllabus, not Competency 1 (life). The path keeps the syllabus placement.
- **Sinhala titles.** The textbook's glossaries use a legacy Sinhala font that does not extract cleanly, so no `title_si` values are given. The Sinhala-medium book (linked in the manifest) would supply them.
- **Concept splits.** The path splits two units into two concepts each, where the competency levels clearly separate:
  - Unit 1: characteristics of living things / classifying organisms.
  - Unit 8: generating electricity / circuits, conductors and components.

  All other units are one concept each.
